Home: what SigmaPro promises and where it stands

The first block is the front door of the app. It says in one sentence what the app is for, offers three ways to begin (your data, an example, or finding out which analysis to use), remembers what was analyzed last, sums up its three commitments (accuracy verified against R, reproducible code and complete privacy) and shows where its development stands. Further down it lists everything that can be analyzed, with a link to the fact sheet of each analysis, shows the program’s details and the opening-screen settings, and introduces the sister apps of the suite. If the tab was closed last time with work in progress, the notice to recover it appears here.

1.1What you see when you open the app

SigmaPro always opens in this block, after the opening screen (section 1.6): when you press Start, the logo and the name travel to their place in the top bar and Home appears. The title sums up the idea of the whole app: from experiment to conclusion, with numbers you can verify. Below it, a sentence says what it does: experimental designs, mean comparisons, regression and mixed models, with an assistant that understands your trial, publication-ready reports and the R code for every analysis.

The illustration on the right is a miniature summary of a typical result: treatment means joined by a curve, each with the letter that shows which means differ from each other, and below them the formula of the analysis that produced them (rend ~ bloque + híbrido × N), with the σ of the logo in the background. It is decorative; the real numbers appear when data are loaded.

The note Your data never leave this browser is a reminder that everything is computed on the computer of whoever is using the app (section 1.2).

What you analyzed last

SigmaPro remembers every analysis computed in Explore or in Analysis: the method, the variables and options, the data it was run on, and when; the same analysis with the same data takes up a single row, that of the latest computation. The What you analyzed last card shows the five most recent ones, and a click on one opens it again with its variables and options. If the loaded table does not have its columns and the data came from an example, the app loads that example again (the previous table remains available under Back to the previous table, section 2.12); if they came from a file, a notice says which columns are missing. The same history, with up to fifteen analyses, is under the History button of the Analysis block (section 4.1).

The history is kept only in that browser and contains no data values: only the names of the columns and of the data. Clear the history empties it.

At the end of each block there are two buttons, ← Previous and Next →, with the name of the block they lead to; in Home only the second one appears, and in LABG Suite, the last block, only the first. The natural route follows the bar: from the data to the analysis, to the report, to the figures and to the export. But any block can be opened at any time.

1.2The three commitments

Below the paths, three cards sum up what sets SigmaPro apart. They are not slogans: each one can be checked; in the first, See the validation leads to Block 8 (chapter 8).

10⁻⁶
Verified accuracy
Each method is compared against R, with data from the literature and with cases built on purpose, before it is offered in the catalog. The rule is that no number may differ from R’s by more than one millionth (relative to its size). A method whose cases have not all passed does not appear in the app. Block 8 shows the outcome of those comparisons.
RReproducible

Every result comes with the R code that gives the same result, with the data, the model and the options that were used. It lets a reviewer, a thesis advisor or you yourself, years later, repeat the analysis without SigmaPro. The .labg project also stores a script that repeats all the analyses in the report (chapter 7).

0Private

Everything is computed in the browser: the data are not sent to any server, not even for the models that run in the R engine, which also works inside the browser (section I.7). Opened with a double-click, the app makes no request outside the computer, except for the first download of the R engine.

A difference of one millionth means that, in practice, SigmaPro’s figures and R’s are the same in every decimal place that gets published. An F of 60.635 in SigmaPro is an F of 60.635 in R; a p value of 0.0034 is 0.0034. When a method cannot reach that accuracy because R itself introduces larger numerical noise (for example, in certain iterative fits), the case carries its own tolerance with its reason written down, and that is how it appears in the validation (chapter 8).

1.3Development status

Further down, the Development status card explains how SigmaPro is built: in milestones, and each milestone is closed only when all its comparisons against R and all its automated tests pass. The list marks finished milestones with ✓, the one in progress with ● and pending ones with ○; the number of each milestone tells which phase it belongs to. All of them are finished, up to the last one on the list: the release of version 1.0.0.

Phase 22.0 to 2.10The models that demand more computation: mixed models (in the R engine inside the browser), repeated measures, variance components and heritability, generalized linear models and their mixed versions, nonlinear regression and dose-response, response surfaces and multi-environment trials with their stability.

The number at the foot of every page of the app (SigmaPro v1.0.0) is the version. This manual describes version 1.0.0.

1.4If there is saved work

While you work, SigmaPro saves on its own, in the browser itself, the data, the analyses in the report and the figure settings (autosave, section 7.7). If the tab was closed without saving the project, or the computer shut down, this notice appears at the top of Home and of Data when you open the app again:

  1. Recover the work reloads the data exactly as they were (with their types, their computed columns and their filter), the analyses in the report and the figure settings. The results are recomputed with those data, so they are exactly the same as before. The app opens in Results, or in Data if the report was empty.
  2. Discard deletes what was saved and leaves the app empty.
  3. If you choose neither and start other work, the earlier work is not lost: the notice stays in view and the work can be recovered while the tab is open. Recovering it when other data are already loaded asks for confirmation, because it replaces them.
!Autosave does not replace the project

What is saved lives in that browser on that computer. It does not travel to another machine, it is lost if the browser data are cleared and, in a private window, it is deleted when the window is closed. To keep your work, share it or take it to another computer, save the .labg project in the Export block (section 7.6).

1.5What you can analyze

The What you can analyze card lists the 78 analyses in SigmaPro, grouped into their 18 families, from descriptive statistics to survival and time series. It lets you see at a glance what the program does before loading any data.

Each name opens the fact sheet of the analysis in Block 9, Fundamentals: what it is for, the model and its assumptions, the data it needs, how to read the result, and its references (chapter 9). From the fact sheet, Open this analysis takes you to the analysis and Load the example data opens it with its example already loaded, so this list is also a shortcut for trying out any method.

1.7Explore the LABG suite

At the end of Home, the black and gold box Explore the LABG suite introduces four sister apps of SigmaPro, the ones that most often continue an agronomic analysis: PCAPro (principal components), ClusteringPro (cluster analysis), AgriDesign (design and randomization of experiments) and CorrelationPro (correlation). Each card shows its logo, its version, its features and the Open app, Manual and Cite buttons; an app that does not yet have a public release appears with Coming soon instead of the buttons. It is the same box as in Block 10; chapter 10 explains it in full (figure 10.1).
